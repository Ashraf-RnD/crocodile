## Why vanilla RNNs vanish: the math

Backpropagation through time (BPTT) unrolls the recurrence and applies the chain
rule across every time step. The gradient of the loss with respect to an early
hidden state involves a product of Jacobians, one per time step:
`dh_T/dh_0 = product of (dh_t/dh_{t-1})` for `t = 1..T`. Each factor's magnitude
depends on the recurrent weight matrix's largest eigenvalue and the activation's
derivative (bounded by 1 for tanh, and typically well below 1 through most of its
range). Multiplying many such factors together drives the product toward zero
exponentially in sequence length — this is not a training bug but an exact
mathematical consequence of the recurrence structure, which is why it affects
*every* vanilla RNN regardless of hyperparameters, only becoming worse with longer
sequences.

## BPTT truncation as a practical necessity

Full BPTT over a sequence of length `T` costs `O(T)` memory (one set of activations
per time step) and `O(T)` sequential compute that cannot be parallelized across time
(each step depends on the previous hidden state). **Truncated BPTT** caps the
backward pass to a fixed window of recent steps, trading gradient accuracy for
bounded memory and the ability to train on very long or streaming sequences. This is
a real accuracy/resource trade-off: truncation means the model provably cannot learn
dependencies longer than the truncation window, which is often the actual cause of
"the model isn't learning long-range structure" rather than an architecture
limitation.

## Gradient clipping: necessary, not optional

Because RNN gradients can also *explode* (the same eigenvalue argument, but greater
than 1), essentially all production RNN training clips gradient norm before the
optimizer step:

```python
loss.backward()
torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), max_norm=5.0)
optimizer.step()
```

Unlike vanishing gradients (a structural limitation that clipping cannot fix),
exploding gradients are a numerical instability that clipping directly and reliably
addresses — this is why clip_grad_norm_ appears in nearly every RNN/LSTM training
script but is far less common in transformer training code, where the failure mode
is different.

## Why transformers displaced RNNs for most sequence modeling

The sequential dependency in an RNN (`h_t` requires `h_{t-1}`) fundamentally
prevents parallelizing computation across the time dimension during training —
every step waits on the previous one. Transformers replace recurrence with
self-attention, which computes all positions' representations in parallel
(at the cost of `O(n^2)` attention compute instead of RNN's `O(n)`). At the scale of
modern datasets and hardware, that parallelism advantage dominates: transformers
train dramatically faster per wall-clock hour despite doing more total FLOPs, and
attention gives a direct (not vanishing) gradient path between any two positions
regardless of distance. RNNs remain competitive only where sequence length is
modest, training parallelism matters less than inference-time streaming, or O(n)
compute is a hard constraint transformers can't meet.

## Comparing sequence modeling approaches

| Approach | Training parallelism | Long-range gradient path | Inference cost per new token | Typical modern use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vanilla RNN | None (sequential) | Vanishes exponentially with distance | O(1), constant memory | Rarely used standalone today |
| LSTM/GRU | None (sequential) | Better via gating, still degrades at long range | O(1), constant memory | Streaming/edge, small-scale time series |
| Transformer (full attention) | Full (all positions at once) | Direct, no vanishing | O(n) without caching, O(1) with KV-cache | Default for large-scale NLP/sequence tasks |
| State-space models (S4/Mamba) | Mostly parallel (via convolution/scan) | Long-range by design | O(1), constant state | Emerging alternative for very long sequences |

## A pragmatic takeaway

Choosing an RNN over a transformer today is usually a deliberate systems decision
(constant memory streaming inference, small model footprint) rather than a modeling
one — the underlying accuracy ceiling for genuinely long-range dependencies almost
always favors attention-based or state-space alternatives.
