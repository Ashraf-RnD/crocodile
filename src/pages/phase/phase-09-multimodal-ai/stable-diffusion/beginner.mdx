## Turning noise into a picture

**Stable Diffusion** generates images from text descriptions by starting with pure
random noise and gradually "denoising" it, step by step, into a coherent picture that
matches your prompt.

Think of it like a sculptor starting with a rough block of marble (random noise) and
slowly chipping away, refining the shape a little more with each pass, until a
recognizable figure emerges — except each "chip" here is guided by your text prompt.

### Your first generated image

```python
from diffusers import StableDiffusionPipeline
import torch

pipe = StableDiffusionPipeline.from_pretrained(
    "runwayml/stable-diffusion-v1-5",
    torch_dtype=torch.float16,
).to("cuda")

image = pipe("a watercolor painting of a lighthouse at sunset").images[0]
image.save("lighthouse.png")
```

`StableDiffusionPipeline` bundles together everything needed: a text encoder (turns
your prompt into a numeric representation), a denoising model (the actual image
generation), and a decoder that turns the final result into a viewable image.

### Controlling how many refinement steps

```python
image = pipe(
    "a watercolor painting of a lighthouse at sunset",
    num_inference_steps=30,   # more steps = more refinement, slower
    guidance_scale=7.5,       # how strictly to follow the prompt
).images[0]
```

More denoising steps generally means a cleaner, more detailed result, up to a point of
diminishing returns — most models look nearly identical at 30 steps vs 50.

## Try it yourself

Use a **negative prompt** to tell the model what to avoid (e.g. blurry, low quality)
without changing your main prompt.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

```python
image = pipe(
    "a watercolor painting of a lighthouse at sunset",
    negative_prompt="blurry, low quality, distorted",
    num_inference_steps=30,
).images[0]
```

`negative_prompt` steers generation away from those concepts during the same denoising
process — it's one of the simplest, highest-impact quality knobs available.

</details>
