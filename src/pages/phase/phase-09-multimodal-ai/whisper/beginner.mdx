## Turning speech into text

**Whisper** is OpenAI's speech recognition model — you give it an audio file, it gives
you back the transcribed text, and it works across dozens of languages without you
telling it which one is being spoken.

Think of it as an extremely well-traveled stenographer: it's listened to hundreds of
thousands of hours of speech across many languages and accents, so it can transcribe
almost anything you throw at it, from a clean podcast recording to a noisy phone call.

### Your first transcription

```python
import whisper

model = whisper.load_model("base")
result = model.transcribe("meeting.mp3")

print(result["text"])
```

That's the whole thing. `whisper.load_model("base")` downloads a mid-sized pretrained
model (there are five main sizes: `tiny`, `base`, `small`, `medium`, `large`) and
`transcribe` handles chunking the audio, running inference, and stitching the text back
together.

### Getting timestamps

```python
for segment in result["segments"]:
    print(f"[{segment['start']:.1f}s - {segment['end']:.1f}s] {segment['text']}")
```

Each segment comes with start/end timestamps — this is what powers subtitle generation
and "jump to this point in the audio" features.

## Try it yourself

Whisper can also translate non-English speech directly into English text, instead of
just transcribing it in its original language. Look up the `task` parameter to
`transcribe()`.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

```python
result = model.transcribe("french_interview.mp3", task="translate")
print(result["text"])  # English text, even though the audio is French
```

</details>
