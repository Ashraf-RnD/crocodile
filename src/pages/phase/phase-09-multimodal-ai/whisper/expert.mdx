## Model size is a WER/latency/cost triangle

| Model | Params | Relative speed | English WER (approx) | Multilingual quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tiny | 39M | ~32x realtime (GPU) | Highest error | Weak |
| base | 74M | ~16x realtime | Moderate error | Moderate |
| small | 244M | ~6x realtime | Lower error | Good |
| medium | 769M | ~2x realtime | Low error | Strong |
| large-v3 | 1.5B | ~1x realtime | Lowest error | Strongest |

Most production systems don't default to `large-v3` — the accuracy gain from `medium`
to `large-v3` is often a few percentage points of Word Error Rate, while the compute
cost roughly doubles. The right choice depends entirely on the downstream use: a
voice assistant needs `small`/`base`-tier latency; a legal transcription service where
every word matters justifies `large-v3` even at higher cost.

## The hallucination failure mode

Whisper's most cited production failure isn't misheard words — it's **hallucinated
text during silence or non-speech audio** (background music, breathing, room tone).
Because Whisper is trained as a language model over audio, when there's no real signal
to condition on, it sometimes falls back on plausible-sounding but entirely fabricated
phrases (repeated stock phrases like "Thank you for watching" have been documented
appearing in silent audio). This is precisely why VAD pre-filtering isn't just a speed
optimization in production — it's a correctness safeguard, removing the silent
stretches where hallucination is most likely to occur.

## Diarization: Whisper doesn't know who's speaking

Whisper transcribes *what* was said, not *who* said it — it has no built-in speaker
identity. Production meeting-transcription tools bolt on a separate **diarization**
model (e.g. pyannote.audio) that segments audio by speaker turn, then merge the two
outputs by timestamp:

```python
from pyannote.audio import Pipeline

diarization = Pipeline.from_pretrained("pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1")
diar_result = diarization("meeting.mp3")

# merge diar_result speaker turns with Whisper's timestamped segments
# by overlapping time ranges
```

This two-model combination (ASR + diarization) is standard architecture for any
"who said what" transcription product — there's no single open model that reliably
does both well in one pass.

## Engine comparison for production deployment

| Option | Self-hosted | Latency | Cost model | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| openai-whisper (reference) | Yes | Slowest | Compute only | Simplest to get running, not optimized |
| faster-whisper | Yes | Fast | Compute only | CTranslate2 backend, standard production choice |
| whisper.cpp | Yes | Fast, CPU-friendly | Compute only | Good for on-device/edge (mobile, embedded) |
| Deepgram / AssemblyAI | No | Very fast, streaming-native | Per-minute API | Best for real-time streaming, no infra to manage |

Self-hosting wins on cost at volume and on data residency (audio never leaves your
infrastructure — a real requirement for healthcare/legal use cases); hosted APIs win
on true low-latency streaming, which is a genuinely hard engineering problem to
replicate with self-hosted Whisper variants.
