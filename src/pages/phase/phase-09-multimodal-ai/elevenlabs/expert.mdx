## The cost/quality/latency triangle vs self-hosted TTS

| Option | Voice quality | Latency | Cost model | Self-hosted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ElevenLabs | Very high, most natural prosody | Low (flash models) | Per-character API pricing | No |
| Kokoro / XTTS / Bark (open) | Good, improving fast | Depends on hardware | Compute only | Yes |
| Cloud TTS (Google/Azure/AWS) | Good, less expressive | Low | Per-character API pricing | No |

ElevenLabs' quality edge comes largely from prosody modeling — pacing, emphasis, and
emotional inflection that make output sound less "read aloud" and more "spoken
naturally." That gap has narrowed against the best open models but hasn't closed,
which is why high-visibility production voice products (narration, branded voice
assistants) still lean toward it despite the higher per-character cost compared to
self-hosting.

## Latency budget for a real-time voice agent

A voice agent's perceived responsiveness is the sum of every stage in the pipeline,
and TTS is only one link:

```
user stops speaking → VAD detects end of turn (~100-300ms)
→ ASR transcribes (~100-300ms, or streaming/incremental)
→ LLM generates first tokens (~200-500ms to first token)
→ TTS synthesizes first audio chunk (~100-300ms with streaming models)
→ audio playback starts
```

Total time-to-first-audio in a well-optimized pipeline is often 700ms-1.5s — every
stage needs to stream rather than wait for completion, which is why `eleven_flash_v2_5`
(optimized specifically for time-to-first-byte) exists as a distinct product tier from
ElevenLabs' higher-fidelity default models: production voice agents will trade audio
quality for latency far more aggressively than an audiobook pipeline would.

## Consent, cloning, and watermarking

Voice cloning raises real misuse risk — impersonation, fraud (voice-based scams
targeting family members), and unauthorized commercial use of a person's likeness.
ElevenLabs requires consent verification for cloning at higher tiers, and ships
**AI-generated audio watermarking** (imperceptible signal embedded in output audio
that identifies it as AI-generated) specifically to support detection tooling —
production systems handling cloned or synthetic voices at any scale should treat
consent verification and detectability as a compliance requirement, not an optional
feature, given the fraud and deepfake landscape this technology sits in.

## When self-hosting wins despite lower quality

At very high volume (millions of characters/day), the per-character API cost of a
hosted service can exceed the fully-loaded cost of running an open-source model like
Kokoro or XTTS on owned/rented GPU capacity — and self-hosting also removes any
dependency on a third party for data residency-sensitive use cases (healthcare,
government). The trade is explicit: lower voice quality and more engineering
ownership, in exchange for cost control and data control at scale.
