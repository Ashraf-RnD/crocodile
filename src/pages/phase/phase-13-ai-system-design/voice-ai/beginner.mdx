## Three models pretending to be one conversation

A voice AI assistant — think a phone support bot or a voice assistant app —
usually isn't one model. It's three, chained together, working so fast it
feels like a single conversation:

1. **Speech-to-text (STT)** turns your spoken words into text.
2. **The LLM** reads that text and decides what to say back, just like a
   chat model.
3. **Text-to-speech (TTS)** turns the LLM's written reply back into spoken
   audio.

## Why speed matters more here than almost anywhere else

In a text chat, waiting two seconds for a reply feels normal. In a spoken
conversation, a two-second gap after you stop talking feels like the other
person went silent and awkward — human conversation has pauses typically
under a second. That means a voice AI system has to run all three stages
(listen, think, speak) fast enough that the total delay stays under roughly
that threshold, every single turn. This is a much tighter latency budget than
a text-based chat product ever has to hit.

## Knowing when you've stopped talking

One surprisingly hard problem: the system has to detect that you've finished
your sentence, not just paused to think. Say "well, I think..." with a
pause, and a naive system might jump in and respond mid-thought. Real
systems use **voice activity detection** (VAD) — listening for a long enough
silence, combined with cues like falling intonation — to guess when it's
actually your turn to hear a reply.

## Try it yourself

If a voice AI system waits a full 2 seconds of silence before deciding
you're done talking (to avoid interrupting you), what's the trade-off it's
making?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

It trades responsiveness for accuracy: waiting longer reduces the chance of
rudely interrupting someone mid-sentence, but it also adds that same delay to
every single reply, making the whole conversation feel slower and less
natural. Real voice AI systems tune this silence threshold carefully, and
some use smarter cues (intonation, sentence completion signals) instead of a
fixed timer, so they can respond quickly to a clearly finished sentence while
still waiting appropriately through a mid-thought pause.
</details>
