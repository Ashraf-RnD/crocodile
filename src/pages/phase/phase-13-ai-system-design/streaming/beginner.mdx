## Why AI answers appear word by word

You've almost certainly noticed that ChatGPT-style tools don't show you the
full answer instantly — words appear gradually, like someone typing in real
time. This isn't a visual effect for style; it's a direct reflection of how
the model actually works. An LLM generates one token (roughly a word-piece)
at a time, and each token depends on all the ones before it. **Streaming**
just means sending each token to your screen the moment it's generated,
instead of waiting for the whole response to finish first.

## Without streaming vs. with streaming

Imagine asking a question that takes 8 seconds to fully answer:

- **Without streaming**: you stare at a blank screen (or a spinner) for the
  full 8 seconds, then the entire answer appears at once.
- **With streaming**: you start seeing the first words after maybe half a
  second, and the rest fills in while you're already reading.

Both take the same total time to finish, but streaming makes the *wait
before anything happens* — called **time to first token** — dramatically
shorter, which is what actually determines whether an app feels responsive.

## Try it yourself

If a non-streaming version of a chatbot and a streaming version both take
exactly 8 seconds to generate the same full answer, which one will most
users perceive as faster, and why?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The streaming version, even though the total generation time is identical.
Perceived speed is driven much more by how quickly *something* useful
appears (time to first token) than by total completion time — a user who
starts reading a growing response after half a second feels like the system
is working, while a user staring at a blank spinner for 8 seconds feels like
it's stuck, even if it finishes at the same moment. This is why nearly every
production chat product streams responses rather than waiting for the full
answer.
</details>
