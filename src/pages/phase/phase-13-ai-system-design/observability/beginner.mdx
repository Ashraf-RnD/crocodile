## Knowing what your AI system is actually doing

Observability means being able to answer "what happened, and why?" when
something goes wrong (or even when things go right) in a running system.
For AI products this is trickier than for typical software: a normal bug
report says "the button didn't work," but an AI bug report might be "the
assistant gave a weird answer" — and figuring out *why* means being able to
see exactly what was sent to the model and what it produced.

## The three basics: logs, metrics, traces

- **Logs** — a record of individual events ("user X sent this prompt at
  this time and got this response").
- **Metrics** — numbers tracked over time ("average response time this
  hour," "how many requests failed today").
- **Traces** — the step-by-step path a single request took through the
  system, useful when a request goes through multiple stages (like search,
  then the model, then a safety check).

## Why this matters specifically for AI products

Unlike a login button that either works or doesn't, an AI response can be
"technically successful" (no error, response returned) while still being a
bad or wrong answer. Observability for AI systems has to go beyond "did it
crash" and start capturing "was the answer actually good" — which is a much
fuzzier, harder question that traditional monitoring wasn't built to
answer.

## Try it yourself

A user complains "the chatbot told me something wrong yesterday." Without
any logging of what was actually sent to and returned by the model, how
would you even start investigating?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

You basically couldn't — without a record of the exact prompt sent and the
exact response generated for that specific interaction, you have no way to
reproduce or even inspect what happened. This is exactly why AI systems log
full prompts and responses (subject to privacy rules) rather than just
"request succeeded/failed" — the content itself is the thing you need to
debug, not just whether the request technically completed.
</details>
