## What ChatGPT actually is

Strip away the chat bubble UI and ChatGPT is a **web app in front of a large
language model**. You type a message, it gets turned into tokens, the model
predicts the next token over and over, and those tokens get streamed back to
your screen as words. The "intelligence" is entirely inside the model; almost
everything else — accounts, chat history, rate limits, safety checks — is
regular backend engineering wrapped around it.

A useful mental model: ChatGPT is not one program, it's a **pipeline**. Your
message passes through several stages before you see a reply, and several more
happen while the reply is being generated.

## Why it feels like it's "typing"

The model doesn't generate the whole answer and then send it — it generates
**one token at a time** (a token is roughly a word-piece) and each token is
sent to your browser the moment it's ready. That's why you see text appear
gradually instead of all at once. This also means the servers only need to
hold a partial answer in memory at any moment, not the whole response.

## Why it remembers your conversation

Every time you send a new message, ChatGPT doesn't magically "recall" the
earlier turns — the app resends the **entire conversation so far** (or a
summarized/truncated version of it) back into the model along with your new
message. The model has no persistent memory between requests; conversation
history is just more text stuck in front of your question.

```
Request to the model on turn 3:
[system instructions]
[user: "What's the capital of France?"]
[assistant: "Paris."]
[user: "What's its population?"]   <- your new message
```

## The pieces behind the scenes

- **Frontend** — the chat interface you type into.
- **API gateway** — checks who you are, how many requests you've made, and
  routes your message to a model server.
- **Model server** — runs the LLM and produces tokens.
- **Safety layer** — screens both what you type and what the model produces,
  before and while it streams to you.
- **Storage** — saves your conversation history so it can be resent on your
  next message.

## Try it yourself

If ChatGPT resends your whole conversation history on every new message, what
do you think happens to response time and cost as a conversation gets very
long?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Both increase. A longer conversation means more tokens have to be re-read by
the model (this is called "prefill") before it can even start generating a
reply, which adds latency, and since API pricing is per-token, longer
histories cost more per turn even though you only typed one short question.
This is exactly why production systems eventually summarize or truncate old
turns instead of resending everything forever — you'll see this idea again
under the "Caching" and "Cost Optimization" topics in this phase.
</details>
