## Serving users from nearby, not from one far-away place

If your only servers are in the US and a user in Japan uses your AI
product, every request has to travel across the ocean and back before they
see a response — adding real, physically-unavoidable delay on top of
however long the AI itself takes to think. **Multi-region deployment**
means running your system in multiple geographic locations, so users get
routed to the one nearest them.

## Why distance actually matters

Data can only travel so fast — physics puts a floor on network latency
based on distance, even with a perfect network. A round trip from Japan to
a US server can add well over 100ms just from travel time, before any
actual processing happens. For AI products where every extra bit of latency
is already tight (see the "Streaming" topic in this phase), that's a
meaningful chunk of your latency budget spent purely on distance.

## It's also about staying up

A second reason for multiple regions: if the one place your system runs has
an outage (a power failure, a natural disaster, a cloud provider issue),
your entire product goes down for everyone. Running in multiple
independent regions means a failure in one place doesn't necessarily take
down the whole product — traffic can be routed to a region that's still
healthy.

## Try it yourself

If a company launches an AI chatbot only in a single US data center and
suddenly gets a lot of users in Europe, what are the two separate problems
they'll run into, beyond "we need more servers"?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

First, latency: every European user's request has to cross the Atlantic
and back, adding noticeable delay that no amount of adding more US servers
can fix — that delay comes from distance, not from lacking capacity.
Second, potentially data residency/legal requirements: depending on what
data the product handles, some countries or regions (notably the EU) have
rules about where certain user data is allowed to be stored and processed,
which "just add more servers in the US" doesn't address at all. Both
problems point toward the same fix — deploying in a region physically and
legally closer to those users — not just scaling up the existing region.
</details>
