## Architecture: DeepSeek-V3-style MoE, scaled further

Kimi K2 builds directly on the DeepSeek-V3 architectural template (see the
DeepSeek-V3 lesson in this phase for the underlying mechanisms): **Multi-head
Latent Attention (MLA)** for a compressed KV cache, and a **fine-grained
Mixture-of-Experts** design with shared experts. Kimi K2 pushes the expert
count and total parameter budget further — 61 layers, 64 attention heads, and
384 experts with 8 selected per token, for roughly **1.04 trillion total
parameters and 32 billion active per token** — trained on 15.5 trillion
tokens.

## The MuonClip optimizer

**Muon** is a relatively new optimizer (an alternative to AdamW) that
orthogonalizes gradient updates using a Newton-Schulz iteration, and had shown
faster convergence than AdamW at smaller scale. At the scale Kimi K2 targets,
however, Muon is prone to a specific failure mode: attention logits (the raw
scores computed before the softmax in attention) can grow explosively large
as training proceeds, destabilizing the whole run.

**MuonClip** adds a technique called **qk-clip**: it directly rescales the
query and key projection matrices to cap how large attention logits are
allowed to grow, intervening at the source of the instability rather than
patching its downstream symptoms (like gradient clipping would).

```python
# Conceptual sketch of qk-clip's intervention point
def qk_clip(q_proj_weight, k_proj_weight, max_logit_threshold, observed_max_logit):
    if observed_max_logit > max_logit_threshold:
        scale = max_logit_threshold / observed_max_logit
        q_proj_weight *= scale ** 0.5
        k_proj_weight *= scale ** 0.5
    return q_proj_weight, k_proj_weight
```

The reported result: the full 15.5-trillion-token pretraining run completed
with no observed loss spikes — a notable claim at trillion-parameter MoE
scale, where training instability is a common and expensive problem (each
restart from a checkpoint after a spike costs real compute and time).

## Agentic data pipeline

Kimi K2's post-training deliberately emphasizes **synthetic agentic
trajectories** — generated examples of multi-step tool use, planning, and
task completion — alongside more conventional supervised fine-tuning and
reinforcement learning stages. This is a direct response to a gap observed in
prior open models: strong performance on single-turn knowledge or reasoning
benchmarks doesn't reliably translate into strong performance on
longer-horizon, tool-using agentic tasks, which require the model to
correctly sequence actions, handle intermediate tool outputs, and recover
from errors across many steps.

## Why the architecture choices matter together

Scaling MoE total parameters increases capacity for the kind of broad,
specialized "expert knowledge" agentic tasks can require, while MLA keeps the
resulting KV cache (and therefore serving cost) from growing unmanageably
large at inference time — the same efficiency logic DeepSeek-V3 established,
pushed to a larger operating point, with MuonClip as the specific enabling
technique that made training at that operating point tractable.
