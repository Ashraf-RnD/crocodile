## The problem before this paper

By the time DeepSeek-V3 (covered elsewhere in this phase) showed that a
carefully engineered Mixture-of-Experts model could reach frontier
performance at dramatically lower cost, open-weight models had closed much of
the gap with closed frontier models on general knowledge and reasoning
benchmarks. But there was still a gap in **agentic** capability — the ability
to reliably use tools, take multi-step actions, and complete long, complex
tasks autonomously, rather than just answering a single question well.
Moonshot AI's team, building Kimi K2, also faced a more technical obstacle:
pushing an already-large Mixture-of-Experts model even further in scale runs
into training instability, particularly with the increasingly popular
**Muon** optimizer, which becomes prone to exploding attention values at very
large scale.

## The core idea, in plain language

Kimi K2 pushed two things further than prior open efforts: sheer Mixture of
Experts scale — a model with roughly **1 trillion total parameters**, only
**32 billion active** per token, built on the same efficient attention idea
DeepSeek-V3 popularized — and a much stronger focus on **agentic** training
data: large amounts of synthetic examples of using tools, taking multi-step
actions, and completing complex tasks, not just answering questions. To train
reliably at this unprecedented MoE scale, the team introduced **MuonClip**, a
modification to the Muon optimizer that keeps a specific internal quantity
(attention logits) from blowing up as training proceeds — a fix that let them
train the full 15.5-trillion-token run with no reported instability at all.

A useful analogy: imagine training not just a knowledgeable assistant, but one
specifically drilled on *using tools competently* — looking things up,
running calculations, taking multi-step actions to get a task done, not just
answering trivia. And imagine that, to build a "brain" this large reliably,
the team had to invent a new safety mechanism to stop the training process
itself from spiraling out of control partway through — much like reinforcing
the frame of a building before you can safely build it taller than anyone
has before.

## Why it mattered

Kimi K2 demonstrated that open-weight models could be pushed to trillion-
parameter MoE scale reliably, and that deliberately emphasizing agentic
training data (not just general knowledge) meaningfully improved real-world
tool-use and multi-step task performance — an area where open models had
particularly lagged behind the best closed models. Released with full
weights and a detailed technical report, it gave the broader research
community both a capable agentic base model and a documented solution
(MuonClip) to a genuine training-stability problem at frontier scale.
