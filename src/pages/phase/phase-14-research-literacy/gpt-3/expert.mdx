## What the paper actually contributed vs. prior work

Decoder-only Transformer pretraining and prompting weren't new — GPT-1 and GPT-2
had already established the recipe. GPT-2's paper even gestured toward zero-shot
task transfer. GPT-3's real contribution was an existence proof at unprecedented
scale (175B parameters, roughly 10x larger than any dense LM before it, trained on
~300B tokens) demonstrating that **few-shot in-context learning quality scales
smoothly and predictably with model size**, without any architectural innovation
or task-specific fine-tuning. The paper is, in large part, a rigorous scaling
study: it evaluates a family of models from 125M to 175B parameters across dozens
of benchmarks, and shows the gap between zero-shot and few-shot performance grows
with scale — evidence that larger models aren't just "better at the same things,"
they qualitatively acquire new capabilities (in-context learning) that smaller
models lack or have only weakly.

## Limitations

- **No genuine learning at inference time.** In-context learning is pattern
  completion within a fixed context window, not weight updates — the model
  "forgets" everything outside its context and cannot accumulate knowledge across
  sessions.
- **Inconsistent, prompt-format-sensitive performance.** Results were
  measurably sensitive to example ordering, phrasing, and delimiter choice —
  a fragility that later research (calibration, prompt engineering literature)
  spent years addressing.
- **No alignment mechanism.** GPT-3 was trained purely to predict the next
  token on internet text, with no notion of being "helpful" or "harmless" — it
  would readily complete toxic, false, or unhelpful text if that matched the
  statistical pattern, and had no way to refuse or express uncertainty by design.
- **Cost.** Both training (estimated in the millions of dollars) and inference
  (175B dense parameters activated on every token) were extremely expensive
  relative to the smaller fine-tuned models it was compared against.
- **Static, stale knowledge.** Everything the model "knows" is frozen at the
  pretraining cutoff, with no mechanism to incorporate new information short of
  retraining — one of the direct motivations for RAG (covered elsewhere in this
  phase).

## What came after

- **InstructGPT / RLHF (2022)** addressed the alignment gap directly: fine-tuning
  GPT-3 with human feedback (reward modeling + PPO) to follow instructions and
  refuse harmful requests, producing a model humans preferred even though it was
  much smaller than raw GPT-3 — arguably a more consequential result for
  real-world usability than raw scale. This became ChatGPT's direct technical
  ancestor.
- **Chinchilla (2022)** re-examined GPT-3-era scaling assumptions and showed most
  large models of that era, including GPT-3, were substantially
  **undertrained relative to their parameter count** — for a fixed compute
  budget, a smaller model trained on more data outperforms a larger model
  trained on less, which reshaped how every subsequent frontier model (including
  LLaMA, covered elsewhere in this phase) was sized.
- **DPO and simpler alignment methods** (see the DPO lesson) later simplified the
  RLHF pipeline that made GPT-3 usable as a product.
- **Open-weight competitors** (LLaMA, Mistral, DeepSeek) emerged specifically to
  offer GPT-3-class or better capability without the closed-API dependency GPT-3
  established as the commercial norm.

## How it's actually used in production today

Raw GPT-3 (the 2020 model) is not what powers modern ChatGPT-class products — it
has been superseded by instruction-tuned, RLHF/DPO-aligned, and (per Chinchilla)
more compute-optimally trained successors. But GPT-3's core contribution — that a
single large pretrained model, prompted rather than fine-tuned, can serve as a
general-purpose task engine — is exactly the interaction model behind every
commercial LLM API today. Few-shot prompting (and its descendant, in-context
learning with retrieved examples) remains a standard technique when fine-tuning
isn't available or practical, especially for closed-weight API-only models.

## Comparison: fine-tuning vs. few-shot in-context learning

| Dimension | Fine-tuning (pre-GPT-3 norm) | Few-shot in-context learning (GPT-3) |
|---|---|---|
| Requires labeled training run per task | Yes | No — examples go directly in the prompt |
| Weight updates | Yes | None |
| Setup cost for a new task | High (data collection + training) | Low (write a prompt) |
| Per-request inference cost | Lower (task-specific, often smaller model) | Higher (long prompts, large general model) |
| Performance ceiling on a well-resourced task | Often higher | Often lower, unless model is very large |
| Adapts instantly to novel tasks | No | Yes |
