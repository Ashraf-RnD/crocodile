## What the paper actually contributed vs. prior work

Attention itself was not new — Bahdanau et al. (2014) and Luong et al. (2015) had
already added attention mechanisms on top of RNN encoder-decoders for machine
translation. The genuinely novel claim of this paper, as its title insists, is that
**recurrence is unnecessary** — attention alone, with no sequential state, is
sufficient to reach state-of-the-art translation quality. That single architectural
subtraction is what unlocked full parallelization across the sequence dimension
during training, which in turn is what made it feasible to scale models to the
sizes we now take for granted. The paper also introduced multi-head attention (running
several attention "subspaces" in parallel) and demonstrated it on WMT
English-German and English-French translation, beating prior best results at a
fraction of the training cost (3.5 days on 8 GPUs for the base model).

## Limitations, then and now

- **Quadratic complexity.** Self-attention costs `O(n²)` in sequence length, both in
  compute and memory, because every token attends to every other token. This is the
  single biggest limitation that shaped a decade of follow-up work — it's why
  "long context" was a hard problem for years.
- **No inherent notion of order.** The fixed sinusoidal positional encoding is a
  reasonable but ad hoc patch; it doesn't extrapolate well to sequence lengths much
  longer than what the model was trained on.
- **Purely supervised, translation-specific evaluation.** The paper only
  demonstrated the architecture on machine translation and constituency parsing —
  its generality as a foundation for essentially all of NLP (and later vision,
  audio, biology) wasn't the paper's own claim; that emerged from years of follow-up
  work applying the same block to new problems.

## What came after: the two-decade compressed timeline

- **BERT (2018)** kept only the encoder stack and pretrained it with masked language
  modeling, showing the block could learn general-purpose representations, not just
  translation.
- **GPT series (2018 onward)** kept only the decoder stack (autoregressive, masked
  self-attention) and showed that scaling this block plus data plus compute produces
  increasingly general capability — the now-dominant architecture for LLMs.
- **Efficient attention variants** (Linformer, Performer, Longformer, and eventually
  **FlashAttention**) attacked the `O(n²)` cost directly — FlashAttention in
  particular didn't change the math at all, just reorganized the computation to be
  I/O-aware on GPU memory hierarchies, and is now the de facto standard attention
  kernel in virtually every production LLM stack.
- **Positional encoding evolved**: sinusoidal encodings gave way to **RoPE**
  (rotary position embeddings, used in LLaMA, GPT-NeoX, and most modern open models)
  and **ALiBi**, both of which extrapolate to longer sequences far better than the
  original fixed encoding.
- **Multi-Query Attention (MQA) and Grouped-Query Attention (GQA)** reduced the
  memory cost of the key/value cache at inference time by sharing key/value
  projections across heads — a direct response to the fact that autoregressive
  decoding, not training, dominates real-world serving cost.

## How it's actually used in production today

No production LLM uses the paper's architecture verbatim. What survives
essentially unchanged is the *scaled dot-product attention formula* and the
*residual + normalization* pattern around each sub-layer. Everything else has been
re-engineered for serving efficiency: FlashAttention (or FlashAttention-2/3) kernels
compute the attention operation, GQA/MQA reduce KV-cache size, RoPE handles
position, and pre-norm (LayerNorm/RMSNorm before, not after, each sub-layer) replaced
the original post-norm placement because it trains far more stably at depth. The
encoder-decoder structure itself is now a minority pattern — most LLMs you interact
with (GPT-4, Claude, LLaMA, DeepSeek) are decoder-only, a simplification the
original paper didn't propose but that fell naturally out of its building blocks.

## Comparison: RNN/LSTM seq2seq vs. the Transformer

| Dimension | RNN/LSTM + attention (pre-2017) | Transformer (this paper) |
|---|---|---|
| Sequential dependency | Strict — token *t* needs token *t-1*'s hidden state | None — all tokens processed in parallel |
| Long-range dependency path length | `O(n)` — information decays over distance | `O(1)` — any two tokens are one attention hop apart |
| Training parallelism | Poor (sequential by construction) | Excellent (fully parallel across sequence) |
| Compute/memory cost | `O(n)` per step, `O(n)` steps | `O(n²)` for full self-attention |
| Position information | Implicit, via recurrence order | Explicit, injected (sinusoidal / RoPE / ALiBi) |
| Dominant modern successor | Effectively obsolete for NLP at scale | Backbone of essentially all LLMs |
