## The problem before this paper

Before 2017, the best machine translation and language models were built on
**recurrent neural networks** (RNNs) and their improved cousin, LSTMs. These models
read a sentence one word at a time, carrying a running "memory" forward to the next
word. Two problems fell out of that design:

1. **No parallelism.** Word 10 can't be processed until words 1-9 have been, so
   training was forced to be sequential — slow, and wasteful of modern GPUs that are
   built to do many things at once.
2. **Long-range forgetting.** By the time an RNN reaches word 50 of a paragraph, the
   "memory" of word 1 has been diluted through 49 rounds of updates. Researchers had
   already bolted an "attention" mechanism onto RNNs to help with this (Bahdanau et
   al., 2014), but the RNN backbone — and its sequential bottleneck — was still there.

The authors of "Attention Is All You Need" asked a provocative question: what if we
just... removed the recurrence entirely, and kept only the attention part?

## The core idea, in plain language

Imagine translating the sentence "The animal didn't cross the street because it was
too tired." To know what "it" refers to, you need to look back at "animal" — not the
word right before "it", but a specific word several positions away.

An RNN does this the slow way: it has to relay that information forward, word by
word, like a game of telephone through the whole sentence. **Self-attention** instead
lets every word directly ask every other word: "how relevant are you to me?" and pull
in information from the most relevant ones immediately — no relay, no telephone game.

A useful analogy: picture a group discussion where everyone can talk to everyone
else at once, instead of a line of people who can only whisper to the person
standing next to them. The group discussion converges on the important connections
immediately, and — crucially — everyone can talk simultaneously rather than waiting
their turn. That simultaneity is what makes the Transformer both faster to train and
better at connecting distant words.

The paper's architecture, called the **Transformer**, is built entirely out of this
self-attention idea (plus simple feed-forward layers) — no recurrence, no
convolution.

## Why it mattered

Removing recurrence meant training could be massively parallelized across GPUs, and
any two words — no matter how far apart — were now just one attention "hop" away
from each other. That combination is the direct ancestor of every modern large
language model: GPT, BERT, LLaMA, and effectively everything covered later in this
phase all start from the Transformer block introduced here.
