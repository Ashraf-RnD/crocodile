## The problem before this paper

By 2018, the Transformer had shown that self-attention was a powerful building
block, but most language models built with it — including the original GPT — could
only read text in one direction, usually left to right. That's a real limitation:
to fully understand the word "bank" in "I sat on the bank of the river," you often
need context from *both* sides of the word, not just what came before it.

At the same time, most NLP systems were still trained largely from scratch for
each task (sentiment analysis, question answering, named entity recognition),
which meant every task needed its own large labeled dataset — expensive and slow.

## The core idea, in plain language

BERT's insight was to pretrain a single model on huge amounts of unlabeled text
using a task designed specifically to force **bidirectional** understanding: take a
sentence, hide ("mask") about 15% of the words, and ask the model to guess the
hidden words using context from *both* the left and the right.

A useful analogy: imagine a fill-in-the-blank exercise where you can see the whole
sentence except a few blanked-out words — "The ___ sat on the mat and purred." You
naturally use every word around the blank, before and after, to guess "cat." That's
exactly the training signal BERT uses, at a scale of billions of words, and it's
what earns the model a deep, general-purpose understanding of language before it
ever sees a specific task.

Once pretrained this way, BERT can be **fine-tuned** — lightly adjusted with a
small amount of labeled data — to do well on a wide range of specific tasks:
sentiment classification, question answering, sentence similarity. One pretraining
run, many downstream uses.

## Why it mattered

BERT showed that a single bidirectionally-pretrained model, fine-tuned cheaply,
could beat task-specific architectures across nearly a dozen NLP benchmarks
simultaneously — something no prior approach had done. It kicked off the
"pretrain then fine-tune" paradigm that dominated NLP for years, and its
encoder-only design became the standard architecture for tasks needing rich
understanding rather than text generation (search ranking, embeddings,
classification).

can then be reused for many different tasks."
