## What the paper actually contributed vs. prior work

None of DeepSeek-V3's individual techniques were entirely new in isolation —
MoE, low-precision training, and latent-compressed attention had all been
explored in prior research (including DeepSeek's own earlier V2 report, which
introduced MLA and DeepSeekMoE). The paper's real contribution was **systems
integration at frontier scale**: combining multi-head latent attention,
fine-grained MoE with shared experts, auxiliary-loss-free load balancing,
multi-token prediction, and FP8 mixed-precision training into a single
671B-parameter (37B active) model, trained stably on 14.8 trillion tokens for
a reported 2.788 million H800 GPU-hours (~$5.5M), while matching or
approaching the benchmark performance of leading closed frontier models. That
combination — this many efficiency techniques stacked simultaneously, at this
scale, released as open weights with a detailed technical report — was
without precedent, and the transparency of the report (unusual for a
frontier-scale training run) let the rest of the field verify and build on
the specific techniques rather than just the headline result.

## Limitations

- **Reported training cost excludes substantial hidden costs.** The ~$5.5M
  figure covers only the final training run's GPU-hours — it doesn't include
  R&D, failed experiments, data curation and cleaning, infrastructure
  build-out, or the cost of the researchers and engineers involved, all of
  which are real costs any lab (including DeepSeek) incurs. The figure is
  best read as "marginal compute cost of the final run," not "total cost to
  produce a frontier model."
- **Hardware export-control context.** The model was trained on H800 GPUs — a
  China-market variant with reduced interconnect bandwidth compared to H100s
  — because of US export restrictions; some of the efficiency engineering
  (MLA's memory compression in particular) is partly a response to working
  around that specific hardware constraint, which is worth keeping in mind
  when generalizing the cost figures to unconstrained hardware.
- **Distillation dependence for some capabilities.** Portions of the model's
  training data and later reasoning-focused successors leaned on outputs
  distilled from other, larger models — a common but not universally
  disclosed practice across the field, and one that raises questions about
  how much of the reported efficiency is genuinely novel versus inherited
  from prior frontier models' capabilities.
- **Alignment and content-moderation behavior tied to its origin** attracted
  scrutiny (e.g. refusal patterns on certain politically sensitive topics),
  a consideration for any deployment context, distinct from its technical
  architecture.

## What came after

- **DeepSeek-R1** built a reasoning-focused model on top of the V3 base using
  large-scale reinforcement learning, achieving strong performance on math
  and coding benchmarks and triggering its own wave of attention to
  RL-based reasoning training for open models.
- **Market and industry reaction ("the DeepSeek moment", January 2025)**
  prompted a broad re-examination of assumptions about compute requirements
  for frontier AI, with visible effects on AI-related equity markets and
  renewed debate over the actual bottlenecks (compute, data, or engineering
  talent) in frontier model development.
- **A wave of efficiency-focused open releases** followed, with multiple labs
  (including Kimi K2, covered in this phase) explicitly building on or
  extending DeepSeek-V3's architectural choices (MLA in particular).
- **Renewed attention to FP8 and lower-precision training** across the field,
  as DeepSeek-V3 served as one of the largest public demonstrations that
  frontier-scale training could be done reliably below 16-bit precision.

## How it's actually used in production today

DeepSeek-V3 (and its R1 reasoning successor) are widely self-hosted and
served via low-cost API access, and are commonly used as a base for further
fine-tuning by teams that want frontier-adjacent capability without frontier
API pricing. Its architectural choices — MLA for KV-cache efficiency in
particular — have influenced how other labs design MoE models for efficient
serving, not just efficient training, since inference cost (not just training
cost) is what ultimately determines a model's practical deployability at
scale.

## Comparison: DeepSeek-V3 vs. a dense frontier model of comparable capability

| Dimension | Dense frontier model (illustrative) | DeepSeek-V3 |
|---|---|---|
| Total parameters | Often undisclosed, likely dense or coarse MoE | 671B total |
| Active parameters per token | ~= total (dense) | 37B (MoE-routed) |
| Attention KV-cache memory | Standard multi-head, larger cache | Compressed via MLA, smaller cache |
| Training precision | Typically bf16/fp16 | Largely FP8 mixed precision |
| Reported training compute cost | Not typically disclosed in detail | ~$5.5M / 2.788M H800 GPU-hours (reported) |
| Weight availability | Closed | Open weights |
