## Turning meaning into numbers

An **embedding** is a list of numbers (a vector) that represents the *meaning* of a
piece of text. The key property: texts with similar meaning get vectors that are close
together, and texts with different meaning get vectors that are far apart — even if
the words themselves don't overlap at all.

"How do I reset my password?" and "I forgot my login credentials" share almost no
words, but a good embedding model places their vectors near each other, because
that's what makes semantic search possible: you can find relevant text by meaning, not
just by keyword matching.

## Your first embedding

```python
from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer

model = SentenceTransformer("all-MiniLM-L6-v2")

sentences = [
    "How do I reset my password?",
    "I forgot my login credentials",
    "What's the weather like today?",
]
vectors = model.encode(sentences)
print(vectors.shape)  # (3, 384) — 3 sentences, 384 numbers each
```

Every sentence becomes a 384-dimensional vector. That number (384) is fixed by the
model you chose — different models produce different-sized vectors.

## Measuring "closeness"

To compare two vectors, we use **cosine similarity** — a score from -1 to 1 (in
practice, usually 0 to 1 for text) that measures how aligned two vectors are,
regardless of their length:

```python
from sentence_transformers.util import cos_sim

similarity = cos_sim(vectors[0], vectors[1])  # password reset vs login credentials
print(similarity)  # tensor([[0.72]]) — high, similar meaning

similarity2 = cos_sim(vectors[0], vectors[2])  # password reset vs weather
print(similarity2)  # tensor([[0.05]]) — low, unrelated
```

This is the entire mechanism behind semantic search: embed the user's question, embed
every candidate document, and return the documents whose vectors are closest to the
question's vector.

## Try it yourself

Would the sentences `"The cat sat on the mat"` and `"A feline rested on the rug"` have
high or low cosine similarity with a good embedding model? What about `"The cat sat on
the mat"` and `"The cat is black"`?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

`"The cat sat on the mat"` and `"A feline rested on the rug"` should have **high**
similarity — nearly identical meaning, almost no shared words, which is exactly the
case embeddings are built to handle. `"The cat sat on the mat"` and `"The cat is
black"` share the word "cat" and topic (cats) but describe different facts, so a good
model gives them **moderate** similarity — related but not equivalent, correctly
distinguishing topical overlap from semantic equivalence.
</details>
