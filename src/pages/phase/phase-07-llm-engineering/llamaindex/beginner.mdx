## Think of it as a librarian for your documents

An LLM can't answer questions about your company's internal PDFs — it's never seen
them. **LlamaIndex** is a library purpose-built for connecting LLMs to your own data:
it reads your documents, organizes them so relevant pieces can be found quickly, and
hands the right excerpts to the model when you ask a question. This overall pattern
is called **RAG** (retrieval-augmented generation), and LlamaIndex is one of the two
dominant libraries for building it (alongside LangChain).

<Mermaid client:visible code={`flowchart LR
  Docs[Your documents] --> Index[LlamaIndex builds a searchable index]
  Q[Your question] --> Index

### A minimal RAG example

```python
from llama_index.core import VectorStoreIndex, SimpleDirectoryReader

# Load every file in a folder (PDFs, text files, markdown, etc.)
documents = SimpleDirectoryReader("./company_docs").load_data()

# Build a searchable index over them
index = VectorStoreIndex.from_documents(documents)

# Ask a question — LlamaIndex retrieves relevant chunks and asks the LLM
query_engine = index.as_query_engine()
response = query_engine.query("What is our company's refund policy?")
print(response)
```

Four lines of actual logic: load documents, build an index, create a query engine,
ask a question. Under the hood, LlamaIndex split your documents into chunks, turned
each chunk into a vector embedding, and — when you asked your question — found the
chunks most similar to it and fed them to the LLM as context.

### Why not just paste the whole document into the prompt?

For one small file, you could. LlamaIndex earns its keep once you have more documents
than could ever fit in a context window — hundreds of PDFs, a whole wiki, years of
support tickets. Instead of sending everything, it finds just the handful of relevant
paragraphs for each specific question.

### Try it yourself

You have a folder of 200 customer support transcripts and want to ask "What are the
most common complaints about shipping delays?" Would you paste all 200 transcripts
into one prompt, or use LlamaIndex-style retrieval? Why?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Use retrieval. 200 transcripts would almost certainly exceed any context window (and
even if it fit, it would be slow and expensive to process every time). LlamaIndex
would index all 200 once, then for this specific question retrieve only the
transcripts whose content is most relevant to "shipping delays" — a much smaller,
focused set the model can actually reason over well.

</details>
