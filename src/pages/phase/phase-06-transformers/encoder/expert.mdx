## Pre-LN vs. post-LN: a training-stability decision, not a cosmetic one

The original Transformer used **post-layer-norm** — normalize *after* adding the
sublayer output to the residual stream. This works, but empirically requires a
careful learning-rate warmup schedule; without it, deep post-LN stacks are prone to
training instability, because gradients at the top of a deep post-LN stack can grow
large before normalization has a chance to rescale them.

**Pre-layer-norm** — normalize the input to each sublayer, then add the (unnormalized)
sublayer output to the residual stream — was shown (Xiong et al., 2020) to keep
gradient magnitudes bounded near the identity function at initialization, allowing
stable training even without warmup, and making very deep stacks (dozens of layers)
noticeably easier to train. This is why virtually every modern Transformer
implementation defaults to pre-LN, at a small, generally accepted cost: pre-LN
stacks can slightly underperform a *well-tuned* post-LN stack of the same depth,
because the unnormalized residual stream growing across layers can make the final
representations less calibrated — some architectures add a final extra LayerNorm
specifically to correct for this.

## Bidirectionality is a strength and a hard constraint simultaneously

An encoder's defining property — every token attends to every other token, including
ones "after" it in the sequence — is precisely what makes it unsuitable for
autoregressive generation. There's no way to use a pure bidirectional encoder to
generate text left-to-right without leaking future tokens into the computation for
earlier ones. This is a structural, not a training, limitation — it's why
encoder-only models (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa) are architected for
classification/extraction tasks where the full input is known upfront, while
generation requires either a decoder-only design or an encoder feeding a *separate*
causally-masked decoder (as in T5/BART).

## Disentangled attention and encoder-specific refinements

Not every encoder innovation happens at the objective level (masked language
modeling, discussed under BERT). **DeBERTa** modifies the encoder's attention
computation itself: instead of encoding position by adding a positional vector to
the content embedding (conflating content and position into one vector before
attention), DeBERTa keeps content and relative-position representations **separate**
and computes attention scores as a sum of content-to-content, content-to-position,
and position-to-content terms. This disentanglement measurably improved downstream
task performance over RoBERTa at comparable scale — evidence that even within the
"encoder" architectural family, there's meaningful room for attention-mechanism
refinement beyond just stacking more standard layers.

## Encoder depth/width scaling and diminishing returns

Encoder-only pretraining scaling (BERT-base → BERT-large → later encoder scaling
work) shows the now-familiar pattern: performance improves with scale, but not
indefinitely for free — beyond a certain depth, additional encoder layers show
diminishing per-layer marginal improvement on standard benchmarks (GLUE/SuperGLUE),
and larger encoders quickly become dominated in cost-effectiveness by
parameter-efficient approaches (ELECTRA's replaced-token-detection pretraining,
DeBERTa's disentangled attention) that squeeze more signal per pretraining FLOP
rather than purely scaling depth or width.

## Trade-off summary

| Design choice | Pre-LN | Post-LN |
|---|---|---|
| Training stability at depth | High — trains without careful warmup | Lower — often needs learning-rate warmup |
| Final representation calibration | Slightly less calibrated (often needs a final LayerNorm) | More calibrated by construction |
| Adoption in modern encoders/decoders | Default (BERT variants after ~2020, GPT-2+, LLaMA) | Original Transformer, original BERT |

## The practical engineering takeaway

Treating "the encoder" as a fixed, settled architecture undersells how much
production-relevant refinement has happened inside it — normalization placement
(pre- vs. post-LN), how position is fused into attention (additive vs. disentangled),
and pretraining objective choice (MLM vs. replaced-token detection) all meaningfully
move the needle on downstream task quality per unit of pretraining compute, even
though the high-level "stack of self-attention + feed-forward blocks" story stays the
same across all of them.
