## BERT: a fill-in-the-blank expert

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) is a model built
entirely from **encoder** layers — the "understand, don't generate" half of the
Transformer you may have already met. It was trained on one deceptively simple game:
given a sentence with some words hidden, guess the hidden words.

"The cat sat on the [MASK]." → BERT learns to predict "mat," "chair," "floor," etc.,
by looking at words on **both sides** of the blank — this is what "bidirectional"
means. Unlike GPT, which only ever looks backward while generating, BERT gets to see
the whole sentence, blank and all, in one shot.

This training game is called **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)**, and it forces BERT
to build a genuinely deep understanding of how words relate to their full context —
which turns out to transfer extremely well to tasks like sentiment analysis, question
answering, and named entity recognition.

### Using a pretrained BERT in three lines

```python
from transformers import pipeline

fill_mask = pipeline("fill-mask", model="bert-base-uncased")
results = fill_mask("The capital of France is [MASK].")

for r in results[:3]:
    print(f"{r['token_str']:>10}  (score: {r['score']:.3f})")
# paris       (score: 0.68-ish)
# lyon        (score: much lower)
# french      (score: much lower)
```

Notice you didn't train anything — `bert-base-uncased` was pretrained by Google on a
huge amount of text and is simply being asked to play its original fill-in-the-blank
game on a new sentence.

## Try it yourself

Try changing the sentence to `"I love eating [MASK] for breakfast."` and predict
which categories of words (fruit? meat? something else?) you'd expect to see ranked
highly, before running it.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

```python
results = fill_mask("I love eating [MASK] for breakfast.")
```

You'd expect common breakfast foods to rank highly — things like "eggs," "cereal,"
"pancakes," "toast." BERT isn't reasoning about nutrition; it's recalling which
words statistically co-occur with "eating ___ for breakfast" across its huge
pretraining corpus, filtered through both the words before *and* after the blank
("eating" pulls toward food words, "breakfast" narrows it further) — that
bidirectional context is exactly what makes BERT's guesses feel sensible.

</details>
