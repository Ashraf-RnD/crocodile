## MLM's pretrain/finetune mismatch, and why it's a real (if minor) issue

Masked Language Modeling trains BERT to predict tokens replaced with a literal
`[MASK]` token, but `[MASK]` never appears in real downstream fine-tuning data — this
is a distributional mismatch between pretraining and fine-tuning. BERT's original
recipe partially addresses this: of the 15% of tokens selected for masking, only 80%
are replaced with `[MASK]`, 10% are replaced with a random token, and 10% are left
unchanged — forcing the model to maintain accurate representations even for tokens
that look "normal," rather than only learning to react to the artificial `[MASK]`
symbol. This detail is easy to skip past but is a deliberate mitigation for a genuine
train/inference distribution gap.

## RoBERTa: an ablation study that became its own model

RoBERTa's core finding was that BERT was significantly **undertrained** relative to
its architecture's actual capacity. By removing NSP, training with much larger
batches, more data, longer training, and dynamic (rather than static) masking
patterns, RoBERTa achieved meaningfully better downstream results using the *exact
same architecture* as BERT. This is a critical lesson for interpreting any
architecture paper's reported results: architecture and training recipe are
separable variables, and a well-known architecture's true ceiling is often
underestimated by the specific hyperparameters its introducing paper happened to use.

## ELECTRA: a more sample-efficient pretraining objective

MLM only computes a loss on the ~15% of masked positions per example — the other 85%
of tokens contribute nothing to the gradient for that forward pass, which is
sample-inefficient. **ELECTRA** replaces MLM with **replaced token detection**: a
small generator model replaces some tokens with plausible alternatives, and the main
model (a discriminator, architecturally similar to BERT) predicts, for *every*
token, whether it was replaced or original. Because the loss is computed over all
positions rather than just masked ones, ELECTRA reaches comparable downstream
performance to BERT/RoBERTa using substantially less pretraining compute — a
demonstration that the pretraining *objective*, independent of the underlying
encoder architecture, is a major lever on pretraining efficiency.

## Bidirectionality's structural ceiling: why BERT can't generate

BERT's bidirectionality isn't just a training detail — it's architecturally
incompatible with autoregressive generation. Every layer's self-attention has full
access to the entire input, including positions "after" the one being computed;
there is no way to causally mask this without fundamentally changing what the model
is. This is why encoder-decoder (T5, BART) and decoder-only (GPT) models remain
necessary for generation tasks, and why BERT-style pretraining, despite its
downstream classification strength, never became the basis for general-purpose text
generation systems. Some hybrid approaches exist (e.g., using MLM-style pretraining
for a decoder that's later adapted, or T5's span-corruption objective which borrows
BERT's masking spirit within an encoder-decoder frame), but pure bidirectional
encoders and autoregressive decoders remain structurally distinct model families for
this reason.

## Distillation: shrinking BERT for production latency budgets

BERT-base (110M parameters) is often still too slow/large for latency-sensitive
production serving. **DistilBERT** uses knowledge distillation — training a smaller
student model to match a larger teacher's output distributions (and intermediate
representations) rather than training from raw text alone — to retain about 97% of
BERT's language understanding capability at roughly 60% of the size and 60% faster
inference. This is a standard production pattern: use the full-size model to
establish a quality ceiling, then distill for deployment where latency or cost
constraints make the full model impractical.

## Trade-off summary

| Variant | Key change from BERT | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| RoBERTa | Removes NSP, more data/compute, dynamic masking | Better downstream accuracy, same architecture |
| ELECTRA | Replaced-token-detection objective instead of MLM | Much better compute efficiency for equivalent quality |
| DistilBERT | Knowledge distillation to a smaller student | ~40% smaller, ~60% faster, small accuracy trade-off |
| DeBERTa | Disentangled content/position attention | Better accuracy at similar scale, more complex attention computation |

## The practical engineering takeaway

"BERT" in production usage today rarely means the original 2018 checkpoint —
it means the *encoder-pretraining paradigm* BERT established, with RoBERTa-style
training recipes, ELECTRA-style objectives, or distilled variants chosen based on
the specific accuracy/latency/compute trade-off a given deployment needs. Treating
these as interchangeable "BERT variants" without understanding what specifically
changed in each is a common source of confusion when comparing benchmark numbers
across papers.
