## Self-attention: a sentence looking at itself

Regular attention connects two different sequences (like translating one language to
another). **Self-attention** is the special case where a sentence attends to
*itself* — every word looks at every other word in the same sentence to build a
richer understanding of its own meaning.

Take: "The animal didn't cross the street because **it** was too tired." To know
what "it" means, the word "it" needs to look at every other word in the same
sentence and figure out that "animal" is the most relevant one. That's self-attention:
same sentence supplies the queries, keys, *and* values.

### The recipe, in words

For every word in the sentence:
1. Turn it into a **query** ("what am I looking for?").
2. Compare that query against the **keys** of every word in the sentence (including
   itself).
3. Use the comparison scores to build a weighted mix of every word's **value**.
4. That weighted mix becomes the new, context-aware representation of the word.

```python
import numpy as np

sentence = ["The", "animal", "was", "tired"]

# toy embeddings, one 3-dim vector per word
X = np.array([
    [0.1, 0.2, 0.0],   # The
    [0.9, 0.1, 0.0],   # animal
    [0.0, 0.8, 0.2],   # was
    [0.1, 0.7, 0.3],   # tired
])

def softmax(x, axis=-1):
    e = np.exp(x - np.max(x, axis=axis, keepdims=True))
    return e / e.sum(axis=axis, keepdims=True)

# In real self-attention, Q/K/V come from learned projections of X.
# Here we use X directly for all three to keep the example minimal.
Q, K, V = X, X, X

scores = Q @ K.T                 # every word compared against every word
weights = softmax(scores)        # normalize each row to sum to 1
output = weights @ V             # blend values using those weights

print("attention weights (rows = query word, cols = key word):")
print(np.round(weights, 2))
```

Notice the output has one row of weights per word — each word gets its *own*
personalized blend of every other word, based on how relevant it finds them.

## Try it yourself

Looking at the `weights` matrix printed above, which word do you expect "animal" (row
1) to pay the most attention to, and why might that make sense given the embeddings
chosen?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

"animal" (row 1, embedding `[0.9, 0.1, 0.0]`) will score highest against keys that
are most similar in direction — comparing dot products, it's most similar to itself
(`[0.9, 0.1, 0.0]`), so the diagonal entry (attending to itself) will likely dominate.
This is common in early, untrained self-attention layers: without learned Q/K/V
projections that separate "what I'm looking for" from "what I contain," words often
attend most strongly to themselves. The learned projections in a real Transformer are
what let a word like "it" learn to look *away* from itself toward "animal" instead.

</details>
