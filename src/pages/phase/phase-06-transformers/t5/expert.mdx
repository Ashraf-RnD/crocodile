## T5 as a systematic ablation study, not just a model

The T5 paper's actual contribution is arguably less "T5 the model" and more the
enormous controlled ablation study behind it — the authors held the text-to-text
framework fixed and swept architecture (encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only vs.
prefix-LM), pretraining objective (span corruption vs. alternatives), corruption
rate, pretraining dataset (introducing the C4 corpus), and scaling — a rare
apples-to-apples comparison in a field where papers usually change many variables at
once. The practical value for practitioners is that many "obvious" architecture
questions (does encoder-decoder beat decoder-only for a given task class? does span
corruption beat token masking?) were answered with actual controlled evidence rather
than folklore, at the cost the paper itself acknowledges: findings were established
at a specific scale and may not hold identically at every scale.

## Why encoder-decoder despite decoder-only's later dominance

T5's encoder-decoder split is a deliberate inductive bias: the encoder handles input
understanding with full bidirectional context, while the decoder only needs to model
the (typically much shorter) output distribution conditioned on that fully-processed
input. For genuinely asymmetric tasks — long source document, short target summary,
or a translation pair — this split can be more parameter-efficient than a
decoder-only model that must re-derive bidirectional-style understanding of the
prompt purely through causal self-attention over it. The trade-off is architectural
and infrastructural complexity: two attention types per decoder layer
(self + cross), two separate parameter pools, and less natural support for
open-ended multi-turn interaction, where "input" and "output" aren't cleanly
separable the way they are in translation/summarization.

## Relative position bias instead of the original sinusoidal encoding

T5 doesn't use the original Transformer's sinusoidal positional encoding at all — it
uses a **learned relative position bias** added directly to attention scores, binned
into buckets by relative distance (with coarser buckets for larger distances). This
is conceptually closer to ALiBi than to sinusoidal/learned-absolute schemes, and it
reflects an early recognition (predating ALiBi and RoPE's popularity) that relative
position information is often more useful to the attention computation than absolute
position — the model cares much more about "how far apart are these two tokens"
than "what is this token's absolute index."

## The 11B model and multi-task pretraining mixture ratios

T5-11B demonstrated the encoder-decoder paradigm scales competitively with
decoder-only models of similar compute class on the benchmarks the paper evaluated
(GLUE, SuperGLUE, SQuAD, summarization). A subtler contribution is the paper's
analysis of **pretraining data mixture strategies** for multi-task training — naive
uniform task sampling underperforms carefully tuned mixture rates that account for
dataset size disparities, a finding that generalized directly into later
instruction-tuning mixture design (FLAN, T0) where balancing hundreds of
heterogeneous task datasets is a first-order design decision, not an afterthought.

## FLAN-T5: instruction tuning as a near-free upgrade

FLAN-T5 fine-tunes the base T5 checkpoints on a large, diverse mixture of
instruction-phrased tasks (1800+ tasks in the FLAN collection). The measured result:
substantial zero-shot and few-shot improvement over base T5 on held-out task types,
at a fine-tuning compute cost that's small relative to the original pretraining run.
This is a recurring pattern worth internalizing broadly: instruction tuning is
usually one of the highest quality-per-compute-dollar investments available on top
of an already-pretrained base model, which is why virtually every production model
release today ships an instruction-tuned variant rather than the raw pretrained
checkpoint.

## Trade-off summary

| | T5 (encoder-decoder) | Decoder-only (GPT-style) |
|---|---|---|
| Positional scheme | Learned relative position bias | Learned absolute (older) or RoPE (modern) |
| Best suited for | Well-defined text-in/text-out tasks with moderate data | Open-ended generation, in-context learning, chat |
| Parameter efficiency for asymmetric I/O | Potentially higher (bidirectional encoder for input) | Lower (input processed causally too) |
| Infrastructure complexity | Higher (two stacks, cross-attention) | Lower (single stack) |
| Dominant use in 2024+ general-purpose LLMs | Niche/specialized | Near-universal |

## The practical engineering takeaway

T5's lasting influence is less about its specific architecture winning out (it
didn't, for general-purpose LLMs) and more about the "unify everything as text
transformation" framing and the rigorous scaling/ablation methodology it
popularized — both of which shaped how the field subsequently reasoned about
instruction tuning, task unification, and controlled architecture comparisons, well
beyond T5-derived models specifically.
